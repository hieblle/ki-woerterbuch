---
title: Transparency Obligation
description: The duty to disclose, in certain cases, that content comes from AI or that a chatbot is answering
aliases: [Labelling obligation, Disclosure requirement, Kennzeichnungspflicht]
---

The transparency obligation requires that in certain situations people are told they are dealing with AI. It is set out in the [EU AI Act](./EU AI Act.md) and mainly concerns three cases: [chatbots](./Chatbot.md), deceptively realistic images or voices, and AI-generated texts on matters of public interest.

For chatbots, the rule is simple. When customers chat or speak on the phone with an AI system, they must be able to recognise that, unless it is obvious anyway. A notice such as “You are talking to a digital assistant” is generally enough. This also applies to applicants who write to an AI system on the careers page.

For [deepfakes](./Deepfake.md), meaning AI-generated images, videos or voices that look or sound real, it must be visible that they are artificial. For companies this becomes relevant when marketing uses an AI voice for an explainer video or an AI image shows a real-looking scene. Providers must also mark their outputs technically, for example through watermarks or metadata, so that the origin can be traced.

For texts, the obligation is narrower than many people think. It applies when an AI-generated text is published to inform the public about matters of public interest and nobody has reviewed it editorially. Internal minutes, a proposal or a customer email that you prepared with Microsoft 365 Copilot and reviewed yourself generally do not need to be labelled under the regulation.

Even so, transparency in the workplace is a good habit, including where there is no obligation. If you pass a summary on to colleagues, a short note such as “created with AI, not fully checked” helps. Then the recipient knows how much trust is appropriate. Many companies regulate in their [AI usage policy](./KI-Richtlinie.md) when content is labelled internally, and they require that a person reads anything that goes outside the company first. How the obligation is to be interpreted in detail will partly only become clear through guidelines and practice. If unsure, it is better to label, and when in doubt ask the legal department.

_Common misconception:_ Some people believe every AI-written email needs a notice. That is not the case. The legal obligation targets chatbots, deepfakes and certain public texts.

_In practice:_

> “Do I have to write under the proposal that Copilot did the draft?”
>
> “No, you reviewed the proposal and you are responsible for it. It would be different for the new chat on the website, there it has to be clear that an AI is answering.”
