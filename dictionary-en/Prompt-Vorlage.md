A prompt template is a ready-made [prompt](./Prompt.md) for a task that comes up regularly. Instead of working out each time how to explain the meeting minutes, the quote email or the job advert to the AI, you take the template, insert the current details and send it. The template contains everything that has proven to work: goal, context, [persona](./Persona.md), [output format](./Ausgabeformat.md) and often an example.

This is what a template looks like. It has fixed parts and gaps. “You are an assistant to the management team. Create meeting minutes from the following transcript. Structure: participants, decisions, open items with owner and deadline. Factual, no filler words, one page at most. Transcript: insert here.” You fill the gap at the end, the rest stays as it is. Someone using the template for the first time gets the same result as the colleague who wrote it.

The benefit for teams is twofold. First, time: the groundwork from many rounds of [iterating](./Iterieren.md) goes into the template once and does not have to be repeated. Second, consistency: minutes from sales then look like minutes from purchasing, no matter who produced them. That also makes checking easier, because everyone knows where to find what.

Templates need a home. Many teams start with an intranet page or a shared document, often called a prompt library. Microsoft 365 Copilot has a built-in prompt gallery where you can save your own prompts and share them with colleagues. In ChatGPT, templates can be stored as a [Custom GPT](./Custom GPT.md), in Claude as a project. Both already contain the fixed part. More important than the tool is upkeep: a template that nobody updates will no longer be used after six months.

A good template grows out of practice. Take a prompt that produced a good result after three rounds, replace the task-specific parts with gaps, and let a colleague test it. If they can work with it without any explanation, the template is done. People who collect and pass on such templates often take on the role of an [AI champion](./KI-Champion.md) in the team without noticing.

_Common misconception:_ “A template has to be written by experts.” The best templates come from the people who do the task every day, because they know what matters.

_In practice:_

> “Our status reports look different every week, depending on who made them with Copilot.”
>
> “Then let’s build a prompt template. Word it properly once, put it in the Copilot prompt gallery, and everyone uses the same one. Then it looks the same every week.”
