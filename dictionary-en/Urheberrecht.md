---
title: Copyright
description: Who owns AI output, what you may upload and when similarity to other people’s works becomes a problem
aliases: [Urheberrecht, Intellectual property]
---

Copyright protects works created by a human: texts, images, music, software. For AI output, the first question is therefore whether anyone holds a right to it at all, and the second is whether someone else’s rights were infringed in creating it.

Purely AI-generated content is, as a rule, not protected by copyright in the EU, and therefore not in Austria either. The law requires a human’s own intellectual creation. An image produced from a short [prompt](./Prompt.md) to Gemini or Copilot usually does not meet that bar. Nobody can stop you from using it, but you cannot stop anyone else from using it too. Things can look different if you substantially rework and shape an AI draft. Then your contribution may be protected. Where exactly that line lies is open in each individual case. For logos and anything you want to keep exclusive, [image generation](./Bildgenerierung.md) is therefore a starting point, not a substitute for a contract with a designer.

For uploads, the rule is: what you would not be allowed to copy and pass on, you should not load into an AI tool either. Your own texts, free content and material your company has licensed are usually unproblematic. A complete textbook, articles behind a paywall or other people’s photos are technically easy to hand to Claude or ChatGPT by [uploading documents](./Dokumente hochladen.md), but legally that is often a reproduction that the rights holder would have to permit. On top of that come the provider’s terms of use, which set rules for how inputs are handled.

The third question is similarity. [Generative AI](./Generative KI.md) has learned from large quantities of existing works. Occasionally it delivers text, images or code that come very close to a known work. Whoever publishes that is, as a rule, liable themselves, not the provider. That is why the principle of [human-in-the-loop](./Human-in-the-Loop.md) applies before publication, meaning a human checks the result: recognisable characters, brand logos, quotes without a source and strikingly familiar passages are warning signs. A reverse image search or a spot check of text passages helps. If you are unsure, ask legal before anything goes out.

_Common misconception:_ Many believe an AI image automatically belongs to the company that generated it. In the EU, as a rule, no copyright arises in purely AI-generated content at all.

_In practice:_

> “We’ll take the Copilot image as our new logo, after all we made it ourselves.”
>
> “Only up to a point. As a rule we hold no copyright in a pure AI image, so a competitor could use it tomorrow too. It’s fine as a sketch for the agency, but for the logo let’s find another way.”
