title: AI Usage Policy
description: The internal house rules that define which AI tools may be used in the company and how

---

An AI usage policy is the set of house rules for using AI tools in the company. It answers the questions employees would otherwise ask themselves anew every day: which tool may I use, what may I enter, and who decides when in doubt.

A usable policy is short. Two to four pages are generally enough, and it should be written so that someone in administration understands it without the legal department. Long documents do not get read, and an unread policy protects nobody.

Five things belong in every policy. First, the permitted tools, with account and access route, for example Microsoft 365 Copilot through the company account or access to Claude or ChatGPT with a [business license](./Business-Lizenz.md). That rules out private accounts for company purposes. Second, the prohibited data, meaning what has no place even in the approved tool, for example health data, login credentials, unpublished figures or contracts with confidentiality clauses. Here the policy refers to the rules on [confidentiality](./Vertraulichkeit.md) and data protection. Third, labelling, meaning when AI content is marked internally or externally, usually going somewhat further than the legal [transparency obligation](./Kennzeichnungspflicht.md). Fourth, the duty to review: anything that goes outside the company or forms the basis of a decision is read by a person first, following the [human-in-the-loop](./Human-in-the-Loop.md) principle. Responsibility for the content stays with the person who uses it. Fifth, a contact person whom people can turn to with questions, uncertainties and incidents without fear of getting into trouble.

Also useful are a sentence on high-risk areas such as HR, where approval is required before use, and a note on how to request new tools. If requesting is easy, the incentive to use tools secretly drops.

The policy is not a one-off project. Tools and the legal situation change, so it should be reviewed annually. In larger companies it is part of [AI governance](./KI-Governance.md). In small ones, a document that management signs and everyone knows is enough. In Austrian workplaces with a works council, it is generally necessary to check whether a [works agreement](./Betriebsvereinbarung.md) is needed. When in doubt, ask the legal department.

_Common misconception:_ Many people see the policy as a document of prohibitions. Its purpose is the opposite: it makes clear what is allowed, so that employees can use AI without uncertainty.

_In practice:_

> “Am I allowed to use the free tool to translate the proposal now or not? You are the third person to ask me that today.”
>
> “It is in the AI usage policy on the intranet, section on permitted tools. If it is not listed, it is not approved, but you can request it through the form.”
