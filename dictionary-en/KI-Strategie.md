An AI strategy defines which [use cases](./Use Case.md) a company tackles with AI, in what order and toward what goal. It is not a thick document but, at its core, a prioritized list with reasons. It is often called an AI roadmap, meaning a plan covering one to two years.

A usable strategy answers three questions. Where does it hurt most in the company, for example long handling times in customer service or too much time spent on reports? Which of these spots are suited to AI, meaning text-heavy, frequent tasks with a result that can be checked? And what should be different in twelve months, in numbers that can be measured afterwards? The order follows from that. Cases with high benefit and low risk come first.

Without a strategy, tools fizzle out. It almost always goes the same way. The company buys licenses for Microsoft 365 Copilot or ChatGPT, sends an email with the access details, and after three months a small share of people use the tool regularly. The others tried it once, found no connection to their own work and went back to the old way of working. Measuring [adoption](./Adoption.md) brings this to light, but by then the licenses are already paid for. The cause is not the tool, but that nobody said what it was meant for.

The strategy therefore connects tool, task and people. Each use case comes with who implements it, what training is needed and how results are checked. A [pilot](./Pilot.md) supplies the numbers, the [maturity level](./Reifegrad.md) shows which foundations are still missing. Requirements from the [EU AI Act](./EU AI Act.md) and data protection belong in from the start, not as an afterthought.

A strategy is allowed to be short. Two pages with five use cases, owners, target values and a timeline are worth more than a forty-slide presentation nobody opens again. And it is revised every six months, because the tools change quickly.

_Common misconception:_ An AI strategy is not the decision for a vendor. Which tool to use is the last question, not the first.

_In practice:_

> “We now have Copilot, ChatGPT and Gemini in the building. Do we still need an AI strategy?”
>
> “That is exactly why. Three tools and nobody knows what they are for. The strategy says which tasks we tackle first and how we will tell in a year whether it made a difference.”
