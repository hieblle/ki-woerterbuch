title: AI Detection

AI detection refers to attempts to determine automatically whether a text was written by an AI. The tools for this are called AI detectors. The short answer to whether this works reliably is: usually not.

The reason lies in the nature of the task. A [language model](./Sprachmodell.md) produces text that looks like human text. That is its purpose. A detector looks for patterns such as even sentence length or typical words. A person who writes in a factual, polished style shows the same patterns. Conversely, it is enough to lightly rephrase an AI text and the patterns are gone. Detectors therefore often raise false alarms on texts by non-native speakers and on formal writing, and they miss edited AI texts. Much the same applies to images and videos, see [deepfake](./Deepfake.md).

You notice it when a tool reports “87 percent AI” for a text you wrote yourself. Or two detectors come to opposite conclusions about the same text. What you do then: do not treat the result as proof. Anyone who accuses an employee or an applicant of having used AI needs more than a percentage. A follow-up question about the content, such as “Explain the third paragraph to me”, tells you more than any detector.

For policies, the consequence is disclosure instead of detection. If a company wants to know where AI was involved, it sets out in its [AI policy](./KI-Richtlinie.md) that AI assistance must be declared, for example in reports, job applications or customer communication. That is verifiable and fair. A detector is neither. The [EU AI Act](./EU AI Act.md) likewise relies on a [labelling obligation](./Kennzeichnungspflicht.md) where there is a risk of deception, not on an obligation to detect.

_Common misconception:_ Many people assume that because detectors are sold, they must work. The error rate is so high that some universities have dropped them again.

_In practice:_

> “The AI detector says Ms Berger's application is 90 percent ChatGPT. I'm rejecting it.”
>
> “I wouldn't put any weight on that score, AI detection is unreliable. Invite her in and have her talk about her last project in the interview. You'll learn more that way.”
