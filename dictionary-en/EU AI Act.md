The EU AI Act is the European regulation on [artificial intelligence](./Künstliche Intelligenz.md). It applies directly in every EU member state, including Austria, and sets out what providers and users of AI systems have to observe.

The regulation takes a risk-based approach. The greater the potential risk to people, the more obligations apply. This produces the [risk categories](./Risikoklassen.md): some applications are banned, some count as high-risk, some are subject to transparency rules, and the large remainder is largely unregulated. A chat assistant for drafting and summarising text usually sits in the lower range.

The obligations apply in stages. The bans have been in force since February 2025, and so has the duty to ensure [AI literacy](./KI-Kompetenz.md) among employees. Further rules, for example on high-risk systems and the [transparency obligation](./Kennzeichnungspflicht.md), follow step by step until 2027. Some deadlines have since been debated at EU level, so it is worth checking the current status before you rely on a date.

For most companies, the role of deployer is the one that matters. A deployer is anyone who uses an AI system in their own name, for example an office that works with Microsoft 365 Copilot or ChatGPT. For deployers of low-risk applications the obligations are manageable: train employees, label AI content in certain cases, and use the tool the way the provider intends. Considerably more obligations arise when a company uses AI in high-risk areas, such as selecting job applicants or granting loans. There, the rules call for human oversight, logging and informing the people affected, among other things.

The regulation does not replace other laws. Data protection ([GDPR and AI](./DSGVO und KI.md)), [copyright](./Urheberrecht.md) and employment law continue to apply, and in everyday work they often come into play first. An internal [AI usage policy](./KI-Richtlinie.md) helps bring all of these requirements together in one place. In Austria, the AI service desk of the regulator RTR offers information for companies. If you have specific questions about how to classify a system, ask your legal department or an external adviser when in doubt.

_Common misconception:_ Many people believe the EU AI Act bans the use of ChatGPT or Copilot in the office. For ordinary use as a writing and research aid, only a few obligations apply, mainly training and transparency.

_In practice:_

> “Do we have to report every use of Copilot now because of the AI Act?”
>
> “No, there is no reporting duty for writing help and summaries. We have to train people and follow the policy. More obligations would only kick in for high-risk applications, and we don't have any.”
