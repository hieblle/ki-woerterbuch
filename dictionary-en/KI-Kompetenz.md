---
title: AI Literacy
description: The obligation under Article 4 of the EU AI Act that employees understand AI tools and can use them safely

---

AI literacy means that people who work with AI understand what the tool can do, what it cannot do and what risks arise in the process. Article 4 of the [EU AI Act](./EU AI Act.md) has obliged companies to ensure this since February 2025.

The obligation applies to every company that uses AI systems, regardless of size. So it also applies to a small office where three people work with Microsoft 365 Copilot. The legal text names no number of hours and no syllabus. It requires a “sufficient level” of literacy, adapted to prior knowledge, role and the specific use.

As a rule, documented training that fits the workplace is enough. For most employees that means: they know roughly how a [language model](./Sprachmodell.md) works, that answers can be wrong ([hallucination](./Halluzination.md)), which data they must not enter and when a result has to be checked by a person. Anyone who uses AI in HR decisions or in customer advice needs more depth than someone who uses it to polish emails. What matters is that you record who took part in what and when. An attendance list and the training materials are usually sufficient as evidence.

What is generally not enough: a link to a video with no evidence that anyone watched it, or a sentence in the [works agreement](./Betriebsvereinbarung.md) that AI is to be used “responsibly”. A one-off training session from the year of introduction also wears thin if the tools have changed significantly since. A short refresher once a year is a sensible rhythm.

The obligation is deliberately worded openly. That gives companies room to manoeuvre, but it also means there is no checklist that covers everything. An [AI usage policy](./KI-Richtlinie.md) and an [AI champion](./KI-Champion.md) in each department help keep knowledge alive in everyday work. If you are unsure whether your approach is sufficient, ask the legal department when in doubt.

_Common misconception:_ Some people think AI literacy only concerns the IT department. It means everyone who uses AI in their daily work, including administration, sales and purchasing.

_In practice:_

> “Is it enough if we send the Copilot introduction video to everyone?”
>
> “As part of the training, yes, but we need evidence. Let's schedule an hour, document attendance and create a separate module for HR, they work with applicant data.”
