---
title: Use Case
description: A specific, recurring task in everyday work where an AI tool delivers a clearly recognizable benefit
aliases: [Application scenario, Anwendungsfall]
---

A use case is a specific task where AI helps in everyday work. Not “AI in sales”, but “transfer quote requests from the inbox into a spreadsheet”. The more precisely the task is described, the easier it is to check whether AI really adds anything here.

Good use cases share four features. The task comes up often, otherwise the effort is not worth it. It is text-heavy, because a [language model](./Sprachmodell.md) handles text best. The result is clearly described, such as a summary in three bullet points or a reply email in the company's tone. And the result can be checked, so that one person can review it in a short time before it is used further.

Examples exist in almost every department. In sales, Microsoft 365 Copilot summarizes a long customer call from Teams and lists the next steps. In HR, a first draft of a job posting grows out of a few bullet points. Purchasing has the differences between two supplier quotes laid out side by side. In administration, an official notice is translated into plain language. In production, a shift handover becomes a short report for management.

You can spot poor use cases by three things. The task comes up twice a year. The result cannot be checked, for example a forecast with nothing to compare it against. Or it involves decisions about people, such as screening job applications, where the [EU AI Act](./EU AI Act.md) imposes special obligations and a review by experts is usually required.

Use cases are the starting point for a [pilot](./Pilot.md) and later for the [AI strategy](./KI-Strategie.md). Teams that start with a list of specific tasks instead of a tool usually reach measurable results faster.

_Common misconception:_ Many people treat a department or a topic like “marketing” as a use case. A use case is a single, recurring task with a clear result.

_In practice:_

> “Management wants us to use AI in purchasing. But what exactly are we supposed to do with it?”
>
> “Let's write down three use cases first. Comparing supplier quotes, for example. We do that every week and can check the result right away.”
